Drawing editor: mockups

For the tickets docs/backlog/drawing-1-editor-tools-and-view.md to docs/backlog/drawing-5-landmarks.md. Static mockups, not a prototype: nothing is clickable (the slides in 2.1 animate). Direction A (Foaie) is chosen; part 1 shows it, with B and C kept folded for the record. This revision: no starting-shape chooser (the editor opens straight on the empty canvas), no ceiling-height plate, no "Începe din nou", the tool plate at the top of the canvas so the bottom holds only "Înapoi" and the arrow, no checks before saving, and the distances from a focused window or door to the first obstacle on each side. Every rule behind the frames is written down in part 3. Tools: Selectează and four one-shot tools — Perete, Fără perete, Fereastră, Ușă — each returning to Selectează after one use. Phone frames are 360×740, desktop 1000×640.

2. The flow (direction A)

2.1 First thing on the screen: the slides

The editor opens on the empty canvas and the slides open over it at once, the first time in this browser. Five slides, one per tool: Perete, Fără perete, Fereastră, Ușă, then Selectează with moving and zooming. Each slide plays the gesture on a small copy of this very screen: the camera starts zoomed in on the toolbar, the tool is tapped and fills, the camera pulls back to the whole screen, the gesture runs on the canvas, and for the window and the door it zooms in again on the piece and its plate. On phones the sheet takes the screen from under the top bar down, so the drawing is large enough to follow. "Sari peste" or Esc closes it; the last button is "Încep să desenez"; "Cum desenez" (2.10) opens the same slides again later. The position is a row of short bars, not dots (the round arrow stays the only circle). With prefers-reduced-motion nothing moves and each slide rests on a still frame.

2.2 Empty canvas

What is behind the slides, and what the client sees from the second visit on. The tool plate at the top always shows the same five tools: Selectează · Perete · Fără perete · Fereastră · Ușă; Perete carries the breathing outline until it is first used. Fereastră and Ușă are not dimmed (tapping one says "Desenează întâi un perete."). Undo and redo sit bottom left, "Încadrează" bottom right, out of the way of the thumb that draws. The arrow waits (grey) until there is something to save.

2.3 Drawing a wall

One tool use, then back to Selectează. Tapping Perete turns it on (filled ink); free ends show as small circles. One drag draws one wall with its live length; a wall starting or ending near a free end joins it. On release the tool turns itself off: Selectează is filled again, the new wall is selected (its number framed, a plate with "Șterge" next to it), and one finger now pans, drags or selects. The next wall needs Perete again. Tapping Perete when it is on, tapping Selectează, or Esc turns it off without drawing; a tap that does not drag draws nothing and leaves it on.

2.4 Fără perete (the side with no wall)

For a side of the room that opens into another room with nothing built between them. Drawn exactly like a wall, one drag, then back to Selectează; it shows as a dashed grey line with a tick at each end and has a length like a wall. Windows and doors cannot go on it, and an opening sliding along a wall stops where a Fără perete side begins. Selected, it gets the same plate as a wall ("Șterge").

2.5 Placing a window, and the distances

Tap Fereastră, then tap a wall: a 60 cm window is placed centred where the finger touched (moved along the wall just enough to fit, if it lands near a corner). The tool turns off and the window stays in focus until the client touches something else or picks a tool: a wash, a handle at each jamb (drag to resize), its width chip on the room side, the wall's numbers replaced by one chain: the gap, the window, the gap (4.4), and a plate with Lățime first, then Înălțime pervaz, then "Șterge" — docked at the bottom of the canvas on phones (two rows there), beside the window on desktop. The width is the number that matters most, so it comes first and is also the framed number in the chain: tap either and the same value is edited. It is prefilled with 90 cm, grey italic because the client did not type it; tapping the box opens the number keyboard, and a typed value turns ink. The keyboard does not open by itself. Once a client types a height, the next window is prefilled with that value (still grey italic). Tapping empty canvas or a Fără perete side with Fereastră on places nothing and keeps the tool on; the hint repeats "Atinge un perete". Both numbers are edited later by selecting the window again. The width starts at 60 cm and the sill at 90 cm, and both carry over to the next window once the client types them.

2.6 Sliding a window or door

Drag the opening along the wall. It travels as far as the wall is continuous: at a corner where two walls join it carries on to the next wall (it is always wholly on one wall; it moves over once its middle passes the corner). It stops against another opening and where the wall meets a Fără perete side. At a free end (a wall end joined to nothing) it may go past the end, until its near jamb meets the wall's end: the opening then is the end of the run, and its far jamb becomes the free end. Drawing a wall from there joins it, so the client can go on wall → window → wall. While dragging, the plate hides and the chain stays, counting up and down as it moves — it is what the client places the window by. Past a free end the chain simply ends with the window: there is no gap left on that side.

2.7 Placing a door

Like a window: tap Ușă, tap a wall; a 90 cm door is placed, the tool turns off and the door stays selected, slides the same way (2.6) and resizes by its jambs. Instead of the sill height its plate has Rotește (rotate glyph and word) and "Șterge". Rotește moves the swing to the next quarter, in a cycle of four: hinge at one jamb opening into the room, hinge at the other jamb into the room, hinge there opening out, hinge back at the first jamb opening out. A new door starts on the first (into the room; for an outline not closed, to the right of the wall's drawing direction). Key R on desktop. A door reads like a window (4.4): 20 | 90 | 190 along the wall, and its plate carries Lățime first, then Rotește and "Șterge". The 20 cm gap is too narrow for a number to sit in the chain, so that one steps out one lane on a leader.

2.8 Selecting a wall

With Selectează, touching a wall or a Fără perete side selects it: a wash band and a heavier edge, its number framed in ink, a handle at each end, and a small plate next to it with only "Șterge". Its length is changed on its number chip; dragging the wall moves it, dragging a handle moves the corner, dragging empty canvas moves the view. There is no way to turn a wall into another kind: delete it (or undo) and draw it again. Deleting a wall deletes the windows and doors on it.

2.9 Zoom

Zoomed in on a corner. Phone: pinch and two-finger drag, whatever the tool; one finger on empty canvas pans in Selectează, which is now the tool almost all the time. "Încadrează" (fit) bottom right. Desktop: wheel zooms around the pointer; +, − and fit bottom right. The hint shows once, the first time the client zooms or pans.

2.10 Cum desenez

The same slides, reopened. "Cum desenez" at the end of the hint (and on desktop the key ?) opens the slides of 2.1 again, from the first one, over whatever is on the canvas. There is no second help sheet to keep in step with them. Closing returns to the drawing exactly as it was; nothing is selected or deselected by opening it.

2.11 Finishing

No checks before saving, for now. The arrow ("Gata, salvează planul") is grey while the canvas is empty and available as soon as anything is drawn. Pressing it saves whatever is there: an outline that is not closed, lengths nobody typed (every wall has the length it was drawn at, shown grey italic), a missing sill height. While it saves, the arrow shows as pressed and does not take a second press. The notes about missing lengths and an unclosed outline are gone.

3.3 What else is in the room

A card menu in the language of the appliance screens: a line drawing and a name, seven of them — Țeavă de apă, Gaz, Centrală, Aer condiționat, Șemineu, Calorifer, Evacuare hotă. 3.5 Placing a landmark

Tap the wall it is on: a 30 cm square in the landmark's colour sits against that wall on the room side, with its name on a small white chip beside it. Like a window, the tool then turns off and the square stays in focus: a wash, an ink edge, its plate ("Șterge"), and its distances to the first obstacle on each side — the same near row as windows and doors (4.4), counting openings, corners, wall ends and other landmarks. 4. The systems

Everything the frames above obey, written out. This is the part to argue with; the pictures only show it happening.

4.1 Tools

Five tools, always the same five, in one plate at the top of the canvas: Selectează (the resting tool) and four that make something: Perete, Fără perete, Fereastră, Ușă. Undo and redo are in the plate on desktop, bottom left on phones; the view controls are bottom right.

One use, then back to Selectează. Picking a tool fills it ink; the moment it has made one thing it turns itself off and Selectează fills again. Nothing is drawn by touching the canvas while Selectează is on, and nothing is ever drawn twice by accident. A tap that draws nothing (a tap without a drag for Perete and Fără perete; a tap that misses a wall for Fereastră and Ușă) leaves the tool on and the hint repeats what to do. Tapping the tool again, tapping Selectează, or Esc turns it off.

Perete and Fără perete are drawn: one drag, from one end to the other, joining a free end it starts or ends near. Fereastră and Ușă are placed: one tap on a wall. They never go on a Fără perete side. On an empty canvas Perete carries the breathing outline; Fereastră and Ușă are not dimmed, and tapping one says "Desenează întâi un perete." Keys: V, P, L, F, U, R for Rotește, Delete for the selection.

4.2 Focus

One thing at a time is in focus: the wall, the Fără perete side, the window or the door the client last touched, or the one a tool has just made. In focus means a --wash band, a heavier edge, a handle at each end or jamb, its number framed in ink, its own plate, and — for a window or a door — its distances (3.4). Focus ends when the client touches something else, taps empty canvas, or picks a tool. It survives panning and zooming.

4.3 Numbers on the drawing

Every number is a white chip with a hairline, Figtree 13.5px, with its unit. A length the client typed is ink; one that comes from the drawing is grey italic; one not given yet is —. Tapping a chip edits that number in place.

One lane, outside the wall, 34px out. At rest it holds one number per wall: that wall's length. When a piece on that wall comes into focus, that number stands down and the same lane holds the chain of 4.4 instead — so the numbers never stack up in two places and the eye always reads one line per wall. A number too narrow to sit in the chain steps out one lane (68px on a horizontal wall, 96px on a vertical one, where chips sit side by side rather than stacking) on a thin leader, and two such numbers in a row are pushed apart. The room side of the wall stays free for the piece's plate and, for a landmark, its name.

The drawing area is the canvas minus the plate bands. The plan is fitted into what is left after the tool plate and the hint at the top and the corner plates (and, on a phone, the docked plate) at the bottom, so a number can never end up under the chrome. A number whose wall is off screen is not drawn.

4.4 The chain: what is next to what

While a window, a door or a landmark is in focus or being dragged, its wall reads as one chain of numbers along the wall — gap, the piece itself, gap — with a tick at every boundary and a hairline back to the wall. It adds up to the wall's length, which is why that number stands down while the chain is up.

  • A gap runs from the piece's edge to the first obstacle on that side: another opening or landmark, a corner where the run turns, a free end of the wall, or where the wall meets a Fără perete side.
  • The chain stops at a corner even though the piece itself can slide round it: a number that bent round a corner is not one a client can hold a tape to. So capăt---perete---fereastră---ușă, touched on the window, reads gap | fereastră | gap on that wall alone.
  • The middle number is the piece's own size and is the same value as the first field in its plate; tapping either edits it. A landmark has no size to state, so its square is a break in the line with no number.
  • A gap of zero shows nothing; the chain simply starts at the piece. Past a free end the chain ends with the piece.
  • The numbers are grey italic, since the drawing is what says them, and they count up and down live while the piece is dragged. The piece's own number is framed in ink, because it is the one in focus.
  • A number too narrow for its span steps out one lane on a leader instead of shrinking or overlapping; two in a row are pushed apart along the wall.

They are read-only in these mockups. Making a gap typed — write 100 and the piece moves to 100 cm from the corner — is the obvious next step and the one open question here.

4.5 Plates

White, --line-strong hairline, 4px radius, 44px rows. The focused thing's plate holds only what that thing needs: a wall or a Fără perete side, "Șterge"; a window, "Înălțime pervaz" with its number and "Șterge"; a door, "Rotește" and "Șterge". Lengths are never in the plate — they are on the drawing.

Where it sits. On desktop, beside the piece. On a phone it docks at the bottom centre of the canvas, above the undo and fit plates: a plate 278px wide next to a piece on a 360px screen would always land on the drawing or run off the edge, and a docked plate is also where the thumb already is and where the keyboard pushes it. It is the same plate, in the one place the client learns.

4.6 Hints

One grey line under the tool plate, on a soft paper backdrop so it stays readable when the drawing runs under it. It says the one next thing and changes with the state and the device ("atinge" on touch, "dă clic" with a mouse). "Cum desenez" at its end reopens the slides.

4.7 The slides

Five slides, one per tool, over the empty canvas the first time and from "Cum desenez" after that. Each plays on a small copy of the client's own screen — the same tool plate, the same room, the same numbers, the same hint line — so nothing has to be translated from the slide to the screen.

One timeline, eight seconds, and it starts on the tool the slide is about: the camera opens zoomed on that tool, taps it at once (no waiting), the tool fills; the camera pulls back to the whole screen; the gesture runs; then either the camera looks at the tool plate again to show it back on Selectează (Perete, Fără perete), or it moves in close on what was placed and its numbers (Fereastră, Ușă). Slide five opens on Selectează and stays on the whole screen for the pinch. With prefers-reduced-motion nothing moves and every slide rests on a still frame.

4.8 Defaults and what is carried over

A window is 60 cm wide, a door 90 cm, a sill 90 cm from the floor. A prefilled number is grey italic, because the client did not give it; typing it makes it ink. Both the width and the sill carry over: once the client types one, the next window of that kind starts from that value, still grey italic until it is typed on that window too. Most homes repeat their windows, so the second one is usually right already.

4.9 Landmarks

Landmarks are what the room has that the plan cannot show: water, gas, the boiler, air conditioning, a fireplace, a radiator, the hood's outlet. They are placed after the plan is finished, one kind at a time, from the card menu (3.3).

  • The tool plate changes. During this step it holds Selectează and the one landmark that was chosen. Walls, windows and doors are drawn as they are but cannot be selected or changed; to change them the client goes back to the plan step.
  • Placing is a tap on a wall, like a window: a 30 cm square in the landmark's colour, against that wall, on the room side. Then the tool turns off and the square is in focus.
  • Dragging along the wall moves it, round corners like an opening; dragging it across the wall puts it on the other face. The face is part of the answer: a radiator under the window is on the room side, the gas riser may be behind the wall.
  • Numbers. In focus it gets the same distances as a window or a door (4.4), and other landmarks count as obstacles. Landmarks have no width to show; the square is a fixed 30 cm on the plan whatever the zoom.
  • Its name is on a small white chip beside the square, in the same colour, on the room side, so a plan full of squares can be read without a legend.
  • More than one of a kind is allowed; the card counts them.
  • Colour is the exception. `design.md` allows ink, greys and photographs only. Seven landmarks that must be told apart at a glance is the one case that earns colour, and the palette is muted enough to sit on paper. The colour appears only on the plan: the square, its name chip, and the small square that stands for the landmark in the tool plate. The cards in the menu stay ink on paper, like every other object drawing. Nothing else in the product gains a colour.

4.10 Saving

No checks. The arrow is grey while the canvas is empty and available as soon as anything is drawn. It saves what is there: an outline that is not closed, lengths nobody typed, a sill nobody changed. While it saves it shows as pressed and takes no second press.
